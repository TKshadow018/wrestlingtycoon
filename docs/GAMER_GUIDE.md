# Player Guide

Welcome to Wrestling Tycoon. You are running a wrestling promotion: build a roster, put on shows, grow your audience, and keep the business financially healthy. There is no single scripted ending, so aim for steady growth and a promotion that can keep operating.

## Your first 21 days

Every new game starts with a 21-day rescue challenge. It is a useful first objective and a guided introduction to the dashboard.

1. **Build a show-ready roster.** Hire at least 12 wrestlers, one general staff member, one referee, and one announcer. Managers are optional. The staffing screen lists available candidates and their abilities and contract offers.
2. **Run three successful Regular Weekly shows.** Check the calendar and open event setup when a show is due. Custom events do not count toward this objective.
3. **Stabilize the promotion.** Gain at least 350 fans above your starting total and keep cumulative operating cash flow at or above zero for the challenge period.

Completing all three objectives by the deadline awards $80,000 and 4 prestige. Missing the deadline costs 250 fans and 3 prestige. Hiring and event-booking transactions are separate from the challenge's operating cash-flow calculation, so watch both your current balance and operating results.

## The daily loop

The game advances one day at a time. Review the dashboard before proceeding: it may prompt you to handle an event, an interview, or a board meeting. A Regular Weekly show is scheduled on Fridays according to the game's calendar; a scheduled custom event can take precedence.

Scheduling an event does not run it. When an event is due, finish its setup and submit the card to resolve it. If you do not meet the event staffing minimums, you can return to hiring or skip the show; skipping forfeits that event's gate and performance effects and applies a prestige penalty.

## Hiring and roster planning

- Start by meeting the event minimums, then add wrestlers whose skills, popularity, and contract costs fit your plans.
- A worker's overall skill is not the same as popularity: skill informs performance, while popularity contributes to audience draw. Compare ability details rather than hiring on the displayed tier alone.
- Review both monthly salary and per-appearance fees. Hiring costs one negotiated monthly salary up front; recurring salary is charged on 30-day contract anniversaries. Wrestlers earn per-match fees, while support employees earn appearance fees when they take part in event segments.
- Keep wrestlers rested. Stamina is spent in matches, recovers daily, and very low stamina can lead to injury. Injured wrestlers cannot wrestle until they recover.
- Track happiness for wrestlers and general staff. Unresolved demands and very low happiness can lead to departures.
- Assign general staff to departments in Manage Employees to gain bounded operational benefits.

See the [people guides](people/README.md) for the responsibilities and useful attributes of each role.

## Booking a show

1. Review the event type, date, card capacity, and any booking or production costs before committing.
2. Fill every segment slot. Choose a segment type and the required participants and support employees.
3. Build a balanced card. Matches and title matches need wrestlers and a referee; main events and interviews also need an announcer. Interviews and promos can feature wrestlers or managers.
4. For matches, choose simulation or match planning. In planning, set the winner, finish, pace, duration, and ordered moments. Check stamina and injury warnings before submitting.
5. Choose a ticket price thoughtfully. The recommended price responds to audience size, prestige, recent event ratings, rival performance, and event type; pricing above demand can reduce attendance.
6. Submit the card on the event day and review the event result. Ratings, attendance, fans, prestige, rivalries, sponsors, and costs all contribute to the result; a good rating does not guarantee every metric increases.

For full segment rules and match execution, see [Events and Matches](EVENTS_AND_MATCHES.md) and [Match Planning](MATCH_PLANNING.md).

## Keep the business healthy

- Check the cash balance and Financial Control regularly. Event gates are not guaranteed income, and there is no flat daily base-income payment.
- Daily income can include subscriptions and recurring sponsors. Daily costs can include operating expenses, contract payroll on salary anniversaries, and other costs. Events also add production costs and appearance fees.
- Consider a smaller or less frequent custom event if your cash is tight. Custom events have booking setup fees as well as production costs when they run.
- Manage sponsor trust and be mindful of a sponsor's brand fit when planning events and interviews.
- Board meetings happen monthly. Review the financial report, make decisions with their cash cost and Relation impact in mind, and complete any mandates you accept. Three consecutive negative operating periods end the game.
- Rival promotions can sign talent, counter-program your shows, and affect audience and prestige. The Competition Desk helps you follow their activity.

See [Finance and Money](FINANCE_MONEY_SYSTEM.md), [Board Meetings](BOARD_MEETING_PLAN.md), and [Progression and Competitor World](PROGRESSION_AND_WORLD.md) for details.

## Grow beyond the challenge

Career arcs last 56 in-game days and offer goals for audience growth, prestige, or profit stability. Completing arcs awards rewards and unlocks, including venues, promotional options, and wrestlers. Build teams and factions, develop rivalries, compete for titles, and use the Competition Desk to follow the wider wrestling world.
