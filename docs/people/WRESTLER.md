# Wrestlers

Wrestlers perform matches and are the core of your in-ring roster. They are hired from the employee market and can compete for singles or doubles championships, form tag teams, join factions, and build rivalries.

## What to compare when hiring

The wrestler skill score averages strength, technique, high-flying ability, leg stamina, body stamina, head stamina, and popularity. The score is useful for a quick comparison, but it combines different qualities:

- **Strength, technique, and high-flying ability** describe in-ring capability.
- **Leg, body, and head stamina** are ability ratings; current match stamina is a separate 0–100 resource.
- **Popularity** helps draw an audience and is not a substitute for match skill.

Skill tiers are Rookie below 62, Solid from 62 through 81, and Elite at 82 or above. A higher tier alone does not guarantee a win or a successful event.

## Booking and development

- Use wrestlers in standard matches, main events, title matches, interviews, and promos, subject to each segment's requirements.
- Match results affect match history, ranking points, happiness, stamina, rivalries, and potentially title holders.
- Teams are useful for tag matches and have separate rankings. Factions are story groups that can include wrestlers and managers; they are not the same as tag teams.
- Wrestler rivalries and faction story beats can add momentum to events. Alignment (face/heel) is a distinct character attribute.
- An assigned manager can contribute fan impact when appearing with that wrestler in an interview or promo.

## Protect the roster

Matches consume current stamina. It recovers over time, but wrestlers who finish below 20 stamina can be injured and unavailable for matches until their return day. Medical staff can reduce match-injury recovery duration but do not prevent injuries.

Wrestlers have happiness, contract costs, and activity expectations. Repeated losses, inactivity, demands, and unresolved promises can affect happiness and retention. Consider salary and per-match costs together when deciding how often to book someone.
